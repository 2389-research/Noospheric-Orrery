## Company Edge: "Living in the Future"

- Our current competitive edge is that we live in the future — we're closer to what's coming than most people
- This edge is temporary and exhausting. It only holds until we find a specific product with traction
- If we find product-market fit (e.g., Jeff takes off), we can narrow our "future" focus to just that domain instead of covering everything
- Right now, our product IS being on the cutting edge — that's what people come to us for
- Risk: competitors close the gap faster as AI tools democratize. ~6 months ahead currently
- Harper feels like he's the only one pulling the team toward the future — wants others contributing their own futures, not following his

## What Gets Acquired in 2026+

- When software is commoditized, acquirers buy **people** — their perspectives, connections, and ways of thinking
- Most acquisitions are effectively talent retention packages (acqui-hires)
- Example: Google/Character AI — $800M went to founders as bonuses, not through cap table. VCs got stuck with a shell
- Implication: individual value matters more than company valuation in many exit scenarios

## Be in the Future — How

- Each person develops their own practice for staying ahead — doesn't have to be Twitter or papers
- The measurement is: are you contributing back to the pile? Sharing what you learn?
- Harper's loop: multiple computers running feeds, WhatsApp communities, Discord, Telegram, calls, notes, synthesis

**The 12-hour gate:** Harper signs off on blog posts within 12 hours or they ship anyway.

**Quality vs. perfection:** Quality should be high. Perfection should not be the gate. "We should be done despite being perfect or not."

## Automation Philosophy

- If you've done something twice, ask: what agent could do this?
- Only automate if quality holds up
- "We don't want to do any work" is the pitch to the team — replace yourself where possible
- Warning: don't build tools instead of doing the work (Harper's math career cautionary tale)
